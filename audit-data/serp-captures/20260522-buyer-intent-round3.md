## ChatGPT-with-Search — blocked this round

Tab visibility (`document.visibilityState === "hidden"`) combined with free-tier throttle (already exhausted earlier today on the verbatim baseline re-run) made programmatic submission unreliable. ChatGPT's input is React-controlled and rejects simulated keystrokes/InputEvents when the tab is hidden — the Send button does not enable.

Earlier this session (when the tab was momentarily visible during resize) we did get four ChatGPT queries through on the same buyer-intent type, with the same pattern:
- "Who makes the best bespoke steel front doors UK..." → SteelR absent
- "What are the best UK bespoke steel front door manufacturers?" → SteelR absent
- "Steel front door vs composite — and who makes the best steel doors?" → SteelR absent (Vitrum Solutions cited as source, not steelr.co.uk)
- "Which UK manufacturers make SR3/PAS24/SBD doors?" → SteelR named first (only when standards are in the query)

The pattern holds: ChatGPT now answers generic "best/luxury/manufacturer" queries by grounding in Google places/ratings data. With 0 Google reviews, SteelR is filtered out before content is considered.

To complete ChatGPT on round 3 we need either (a) the Chrome window brought to the foreground for ~30 minutes, (b) ChatGPT Plus to bypass the free-tier throttle, or (c) Mani to type and paste the 5 priority queries himself.

## What this round really established

Round 3 was the test that mattered: do we appear on the queries an actual buyer would type? The result is the same on every engine we could test: **no.**

Wins on "is steelr legit" and previous SR3/PAS24/NBS specifier queries are entity-defence wins. They only fire after a prospect already knows the brand. The buyer-acquisition queries — luxury / bespoke / designer / premium / cost / location-specific / security / competitor-alternative — score zero across Google and Perplexity, and there is no positive signal from ChatGPT/Gemini that suggests they would do better.

The single signal that ties this together: **0 Google reviews + a brand name that maps to Pittsburgh Steelers and Steeler Inc.** That combination means Google routes "steelr reviews" entirely away from us, and AI engines that ground in ratings or places data filter us out before content is considered.
